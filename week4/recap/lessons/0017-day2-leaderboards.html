Leaderboards & commercial patterns

Where to look up models — and the three ways businesses deploy them.

Why this matters for the switch: knowing which leaderboard answers which question — cost, niche skill, contamination, human preference — lets you justify a model choice with evidence. And naming automation vs augmentation vs agentic shows you think about business value, not just tech.

Today's win

You can read an intelligence-vs-cost chart, pick the right leaderboard for a question, and place a product on the automation → augmentation → agentic spectrum.

The leaderboard toolkit

  • Artificial Analysis — a composite intelligence score plotted against cost & speed. Aim for the "cheap & smart" quadrant; if a model is beaten on both axes by another, drop it.
  • Cost must include reasoning tokens. A heavy reasoner (e.g. Grok-4) can cost 10–20× more per query than its sticker price suggests; GPT-5 scored high yet cheaper by reasoning more efficiently.
  • Vellum — a one-stop table of API cost, context window, and speed across providers.
  • SEAL — specialized expert benchmarks (tool use, languages, vision, security) for niche tasks.
  • LiveBench — contamination-resistant: refreshes questions periodically so models can't have memorized them.
  • LM Arena — blind, head-to-head human votes producing Elo ratings (~50k votes). The crowd can't be gamed the way a fixed benchmark can.
  • Three commercial patterns: automation (replace repetitive work) → augmentation (human + AI co-pilot) → agentic AI (autonomous, multi-step decisions).
How to actually use them: Artificial Analysis to filter on cost/intelligence → SEAL if your task is niche → LiveBench if you distrust contamination → LM Arena for chat-quality consensus. Different questions, different boards.

Reading the cost/intelligence chart

# Mental model, not code: each model is a point
#   Y axis = intelligence (composite benchmark score)
#   X axis = cost per task  (INCLUDING reasoning tokens)
#
# Prefer: high Y, low X  (top-left = cheap & smart)
# Reject: any model another beats on BOTH axes (down & right)
